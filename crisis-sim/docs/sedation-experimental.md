# Sedation on a natural airway: the experimental model

Model `experimental-0.1 (2026-11-13)`, in `sim/sedationExperimental.js`. Generated by `node tools/sedation_params_doc.mjs`; do not edit by hand.

**Status: experimental.** Off unless a caller switches it on (`SimRunner.setSedationModel('experimental')`). With it off the engine is
exactly as before (the whole suite passes unchanged). Wherever it runs, the room must say "experimental, not reviewed".

Three kinds of evidence are kept apart:

1. **Source checks** (a citation exists for the value or the formula): 6 parameters.
2. **Owner sign-off** (a decision recorded by the owner): 1 parameter.
3. **Unsourced** (a working value chosen so the model moves the right way; must be reviewed before it is taught): 22 parameters.

Software tests (`test/sedation-experimental.test.js`) only show that the model behaves as written and gives the same course from
the same seed. They are not evidence that it is clinically right. **No faculty review has been done.**

## The five pieces of state

| State | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Depth | BIS from the engine's Eleveld 2018 PK/PD with opioid synergy | existing engine (owner sign-off) |
| Effort | central drive = drug depression (may reach zero) x complication drive x CO2 term | engine coefficients (unsourced, ported) + new CO2 term (unsourced) |
| Patency | upper airway from depth and collapsibility, relieved by chin lift, jaw thrust, adjuncts | new (unsourced sizes; lateral direction cited) |
| Effective ventilation | (tidal volume - dead space) x rate x patency, on ideal body weight | Radford and Devine rules (sourced) on engine 7 mL/kg (unsourced) |
| Supplemental oxygen | none, nasal cannula by flow, or the anesthesia mask on the circuit | cannula rule of thumb (unsourced) |

The engine's laryngospasm thresholds (patency 0.35 / 0.05; propofol Ce over 1.5 or succinylcholine to break it) belong to the
laryngospasm model and are **not** used here. The engine's own complication patency still multiplies this one.

## Source checks
| Parameter | Value | Source and how it is applied |
|---|---|---|
| `co2SetPointMmHg` | 40 | Normal arterial PCO2 35-45 mmHg (any physiology text) |
| `deadSpaceMlPerKgIbw` | 2.2 | Anatomic dead space about 1 mL per lb (2.2 mL/kg) of IDEAL body weight (Radford 1955 rule, as in standard texts). The engine applies it to total weight; here it is ideal weight |
| `idealBodyWeight` | Devine: male 50 kg, female 45.5 kg, + 2.3 kg per inch over 60 in | Devine BJ, Drug Intell Clin Pharm 1974 (the formula used by ARDSNet) |
| `apneaRateThreshold` | 2 | The engine's own rule: fewer than 2 breaths a minute counts as not breathing |
| `oxygenStore` | mass balance: d(stores)/dt = VA x (FiO2 - FAO2) - VO2 | The alveolar gas equation at steady state (FAO2 = FiO2 - VO2/VA), standard respiratory physiology. Replaces, in this mode only, the engine's blend toward an empty store by VA / (0.045 L/min per kg of TOTAL weight), which made any hypoventilation in a large patient behave like apnea. Apnea still drains the store at VO2, so the signed-off Benumof apnea times are unchanged. VO2 itself is the engine's 3.5 mL/kg of total weight (unsourced for obesity) |
| `alveolarPO2` | PAO2 = FAO2 x (PB - PH2O), with FAO2 from the mass-balanced store | Equivalent at steady state to the alveolar gas equation PAO2 = PIO2 - PACO2/R (863/713 converts VO2 to alveolar conditions). The engine subtracts PaCO2/0.8 from a store that does not account for O2 uptake; with the mass balance that would count uptake twice |

## Unsourced (needs review)
| Parameter | Value | Why this value, and what is not known |
|---|---|---|
| `drugDepressionFloor` | 0 | The engine floors drug depression at 0.1 (never apnea). Here it may reach 0 so a deep enough dose stops breathing. The per-drug coefficients themselves (propofol 0.6 at Ce 4, fentanyl 1.1, midazolam 0.3, volatile 0.4 per MAC) are the engine's own, ported from the Unity trainer without a citation |
| `propofolDepression` | (Ce/4)^2, 0 to 1 | Experimental mode only: propofol depresses central drive by the square of Ce/4, so an induction concentration (Ce 4 or more) stops breathing and sedation concentrations (Ce 1.5-2.5) slow it by 15-40 %. The engine's own rule is 0.6 x Ce/4 and never reaches apnea. Shape and threshold need review against the FDA propofol label and the literature |
| `co2GainPerMmHg` | 0.05 | Fractional rise in drive per mmHg above the set point when undrugged. Chosen so 60 mmHg about doubles awake ventilation; the real response varies widely between people |
| `co2GainBluntedByDrugs` | true | The CO2 gain is multiplied by the square of the remaining drug drive, so a drugged patient responds much less to the same rise (the direction is textbook; the shape is not sourced) |
| `co2DriveMax` | 2.5 | Ceiling on the CO2 term |
| `tidalVolumeMlPerKgIbw` | 7 | The engine uses 7 mL/kg of TOTAL weight; here it is ideal body weight, so a 139 kg man does not breathe 975 mL breaths. 7 mL/kg is the engine's own figure |
| `tidalVolumeShallowing` | 0.45 | Tidal volume falls to (1 - 0.45) of normal as drug depression takes the drive to zero: drugged breathing is shallow as well as slow; a CO2 rise speeds the rate but does not deepen the breath here |
| `obstructionHalfBis` | 55 | BIS at which a patient with an ordinary airway has lost half the upper-airway flow. Direction (deeper = more collapse) is established; the number is not |
| `obstructionWidthBis` | 5 | Steepness of that loss, in BIS points |
| `shiftOsa` | 6 | An OSA patient obstructs 6 BIS points lighter |
| `shiftBmi40` | 4 | BMI 40 or more: 4 points lighter |
| `shiftMallampati34` | 2 | Mallampati III or IV: 2 points lighter |
| `shiftLateral` | -6 | Lateral position: 6 points deeper before the same collapse. Direction supported by Isono S et al., Anesthesiology 2002;97:780 (lateral position decreases collapsibility of the passive pharynx in OSA, doi:10.1097/00000542-200210000-00006); the size is not |
| `shiftRamped` | -3 | Head-up/ramped: 3 points deeper. Direction plausible; not sourced here |
| `reliefChinLift` | 0.55 | Fraction of the obstruction left with a chin lift |
| `reliefJawThrust` | 0.3 | Fraction left with a jaw thrust |
| `reliefNasalAirway` | 0.5 | Fraction left with a nasal airway |
| `reliefOralAirway` | 0.35 | Fraction left with an oral airway (only tolerated deep; the model does not yet model gagging) |
| `patencyFloor` | 0.03 | Complete obstruction still leaks a little |
| `metabolicWeight` | ideal body weight + 0.4 x (total - ideal) | VO2 (3.5 mL/kg) and VCO2 (2.8 mL/kg) are scaled to this weight instead of total weight. The scalar is borrowed from drug dosing (adjusted body weight); its use for metabolic rate is NOT sourced. Without it a 139 kg man breathing ideal-weight tidal volumes sits at PaCO2 near 60 awake |
| `cannulaFio2` | 0.20 + 0.04 per L/min, 1-6 L/min (24-44 %) | Bedside rule of thumb found in many nursing and respiratory texts; real FiO2 by cannula falls as the patient's minute ventilation rises and with mouth breathing. Not from a validated model |
| `cannulaMaxFlow` | 6 | Flows above 6 L/min by ordinary cannula add little and dry the mucosa (common practice) |

## Reproducible cases (`test/sedation-experimental.test.js`)
- **A, stable:** 70 kg adult, left lateral, cannula 2 L/min, propofol 40 mg then 300 mg/h for 20 min: breathing kept, patency over 0.85, SpO2 95 % or more, PaCO2 under 55.
- **B, deterioration:** the endoscopy patient (139.4 kg, 178 cm, OSA, BMI 44, Mallampati III), left lateral, cannula 2 L/min, propofol 100 mg + fentanyl 50 mcg + propofol 450 mg/h: patency under 0.4, PaCO2 over 48 and SpO2 under 90 % within 5 min.
- **C, response:** B, answered at the first SpO2 under 92 % with a jaw thrust, the infusion stopped and the cannula at 6 L/min: patency over 0.7, SpO2 at least 94 % at 10 min and not under 92 % after the first two minutes.

## Known limits (found while building it)
- **SpO2 below 90 %.** The engine has no blood oxygen store and no circulation delay, so once saturation passes 90 % it can fall to the floor (1 %) within a minute. The signed-off desaturation work fixed the time to 90 %, not the shape below it. Not changed here.
- **Metabolic rate in obesity.** The engine scales VO2 and VCO2 with total weight. This mode uses an adjusted weight (unsourced for metabolism).
- **Apnea times are kept close to the default engine** (time from apnea to SpO2 90 %, seed 4200): 70 kg preoxygenated 485 s default vs 472 s here; room air 36 s vs 49 s; 139 kg preoxygenated 164 s vs 205 s; room air 16 s vs 19 s. The 139 kg difference comes from the metabolic weight.
- Not modeled: gagging or coughing with an oral airway in a lighter patient, mouth breathing on a cannula, the endoscope's own stimulation of the airway, capnography sampling dilution under a cannula.
